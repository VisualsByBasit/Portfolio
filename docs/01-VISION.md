# 01 — VISION

*Tier 1. Changes almost never. When in doubt, this document wins.*

---

## Purpose

This is not a portfolio website. It is a portfolio *experience*.

Its job is not to list work. Its job is to make one person memorable to a stranger who was skimming, and to do it through craft rather than claims.

The practical goal is employment and client work. The creative goal is a piece of work that stands on its own regardless of whether it achieves the practical one.

---

## Creative thesis

**The portfolio is about perspective.**

An overcast layer is oppressive from underneath — grey, low, close. It shrinks the world. Climb through it and it is the same weather, brilliant, endless, and beneath you. Nothing about the weather changed. Only the altitude of the person looking at it.

That is the story of someone who has spent two years building in the dark and is now trying to be seen. Same person. Same work. Different vantage.

### What the thesis is not

**Not "exploration."** Too broad. Exploration justifies a globe, a map, a compass, a submarine, a starfield. A thesis that excludes nothing is not a thesis.

**Not "aviation."** Aviation is the *language*, not the subject. It is how we say the thing. It is not the thing.

**Not "a journey."** Every portfolio claims a journey. The specific claim here is narrower: *the view changed because you moved, not because the world did.*

---

## Emotional goal

The visitor should feel, in order:

1. **Focus.** Enclosed, technical, private. Someone working late. Not sad — *concentrated*.
2. **Ascent.** Something is happening. They are causing it.
3. **Disorientation.** Brief. Reference falls away.
4. **Relief, then stillness.** Not triumph. The correct feeling at the top of a climb is quiet, not victory. Nothing has been won. But you can see.
5. **Curiosity.** They want to look around rather than leave.

The emotion we are explicitly *not* chasing is "impressed." Impressed is shallow and short. We want *remembered*.

---

## Definition of success

In descending order of importance. A lower item cannot compensate for a higher one.

1. **Someone who was skimming stops skimming.** Measured honestly: does the visitor reach the bottom.
2. **The work is understood.** They leave knowing what he builds and how well.
3. **They feel something at the break.** Not "that was cool" — a physical, involuntary reaction.
4. **It runs at 60fps on ordinary hardware.** A beautiful experience nobody's laptop can render is a failed experience.
5. **It is unmistakably his.** Nobody looks at it and names the site it was copied from.
6. **It is shipped.** An unshipped masterpiece earns nothing.

Awwwards recognition is a possible *consequence* of the above. It is not a goal, and designing for it directly produces derivative work.

---

## The Constitution

Permanent. These outrank every other document, every feature request, and every good idea.

1. **Emotion before aesthetics.**
2. **Story before sections.**
3. **Craftsmanship before trends.**
4. **Motion must have purpose.** Anything that moves to attract attention is advertising.
5. **Performance is a feature.** 60fps on modest hardware beats any single effect.
6. **Less but better.**
7. **Consistency over novelty.**
8. **Build environments, not webpages.**
9. **Everything must earn its place — including things already built.**
10. **Challenge weak ideas.** Including the client's. Including the director's.

### The test

For any element:

> **Does this belong to the climb, or to the view?**

If neither, it does not belong. This test is designed to kill good ideas. That is its function — a portfolio of ten impressive unrelated things is a showreel. A portfolio with one idea executed completely is a statement.

---

## Creative references

References are studied for **principle**, never imitated for **appearance**. What we take from each:

**Igloo** — *committed atmosphere*. It works not because it is white but because it commits absolutely to one world and lets every decision serve it. We take the discipline, not the palette. Arriving at a similar atmosphere through our own subject matter is the difference between reference and theft.

**Messenger (Abeto)** — *rewarded curiosity*. Exploration that gives something back. We take the principle that a visitor who pokes at something should be pleased they did. We do not take the playfulness — our register is calm.

**Active Theory** — *world-building and camera*. Space that feels continuous rather than sectioned. We take the spatial thinking. We explicitly reject the performance cost: they have a studio and a technical brand that justifies it. We have one person and a recruiter's work laptop.

**Orano** — *confident restraint*. Premium without shouting. Transitions that feel engineered rather than decorated.

**None of these are aviation sites. That is deliberate.** The subject matter is ours; only the standard of craft is borrowed.

---

## Scope reality

This is one nineteen-year-old, not a studio. That is not a reason to aim lower, but it dictates strategy:

> Solo portfolios are not remembered for having ten impressive features. They are remembered for **one unforgettable idea executed flawlessly.**

Every hour spent adding breadth is an hour not spent on depth. When forced to choose, cut.
